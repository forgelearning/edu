#### Overall Impression

The landing page now has a convincing first viewport: the promise, interactive proof, primary CTA, and inline consent treatment work together. The single biggest opportunity is to make the page feel like one decisive journey instead of a student homepage, teacher sales page, school overview, and product tour stacked into one long surface.

#### What's Working

- The hero headline is specific, memorable, and paired with a real question rather than a generic product illustration.
- The primary CTA clearly outranks the waiting-list link, so visitors can try the product before committing.
- The consent card is transparent and inline. It does not obscure the question card or pretend analytics are part of the learning experience.

#### Priority Issues

**[P1] One homepage still serves three primary audiences**

**Why it matters:** The header exposes student, teacher, and school routes, while the body continues through student, teacher, subject, and early-access sections. A first-time visitor must decide both “who is this for?” and “where should I start?” before the product has earned that cognitive load.

**Fix:** Keep students as the primary homepage journey because the hero CTA and proof are student-led. Move teacher and school value into one quieter secondary route or dedicated landing pages, with one clear handoff near the lower page.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify the landing page’s primary audience and reduce competing navigation paths`

**[P1] Mobile still spends too much of the first viewport on explanation and consent**

**Why it matters:** The desktop proof is immediately visible, but the mobile layout puts the question card near the lower edge of the first viewport while the lede, CTA row, supporting line, and consent card consume valuable height. The product’s strongest evidence can still arrive after the user has already made a judgment.

**Fix:** Keep the short lede, but move the compact question proof directly after the primary CTA on mobile. Treat the consent explanation as a compact row with a disclosure for details, and keep the exam-board line after the proof.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt the landing hero so mobile reaches the question proof sooner`

**[P2] Embedded previews create semantic and interaction noise**

**Why it matters:** The page contains student and teacher dashboard previews with additional `h1` elements, nav controls, and many placeholder `#` links. This makes the homepage structurally read like several pages merged together and can confuse screen-reader users about the document outline.

**Fix:** Convert preview titles to section-level headings under the page’s single `h1`, use inert preview controls where they are illustrative, and reserve real links for actions that actually leave the homepage.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden the landing page previews for semantic structure and accessible interaction`

**[P2] Copy cadence is still too mechanically contrasted**

**Why it matters:** The detector’s 107 em-dashes and 4 aphoristic constructions reinforce a noticeable rhythm of short contrast lines and compressed claims. The strongest Forge lines are good; the accumulation makes the voice feel designed rather than spoken.

**Fix:** Keep the hero and loop language that explains the product clearly. In the mode, teacher, and subject sections, replace some em-dashes with direct sentences and add concrete examples where a claim currently relies on a slogan.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable refine the landing page copy for a more natural rhythm while preserving the Forge voice`

The large page then introduces Forge, Anvil, Crucible, student dashboards, teacher dashboards, subjects, and early access; Jordan may not know which of those matters now.

Casey can understand the promise, but may leave before reaching the evidence or return to a page whose scroll position does not preserve the intended first action.

#### Minor Observations

- The desktop hero currently has a strong visual balance: the question card is visible without overpowering the headline.
- The supporting line “Exam-board mapped practice for GCSE and A Level” is useful proof, but it competes with the consent card for the same vertical slot on small screens.
- The page uses one clear `h1` in the hero, but embedded preview `h1` elements weaken the overall heading hierarchy.
- The subject selector exposes a very long list of subjects; grouping by level or surfacing a smaller recommended set would reduce scanning effort.
- The footer is comprehensive, but it repeats audience routes after the page has already repeated them several times.

#### Questions to Consider

- What would happen if the student dashboard preview became one compact proof point instead of a full interactive surface?
- Can the consent card explain less in the hero while still linking to the full privacy detail?
- Which three product claims must a visitor remember after scrolling the entire page, and what can be removed?
