## The organising idea: fieldwork reasoning, not river location

The Darent case study is a setting, not the taxonomy. The groups target:

- linked downstream patterns in Bradshaw's model;
- the geometry of discharge and hydraulic measures;
- instrument placement and representative sampling;
- safety, evidence display and validity;
- flood-response mechanisms and sediment sampling.

## Grouping decisions worth knowing

- **Bradshaw's model includes channel geometry.** Questions 01, 06, 12, 20
  and FB-02 test linked downstream changes, including wetted perimeter, rather
  than isolated memorisation of one variable.
- **Discharge geometry is separate from equipment.** Questions 05, 11, 19,
  21 and FB-01 use area, velocity, width, depth and hydraulic radius. Questions
  02, 03, 13 and 27 instead test how tools are positioned and what they measure.
- **Sampling technique includes the float limitation.** Questions 04, 17, 18
  and FB-03 test whether readings represent the whole channel and whether the
  method measures surface or mean velocity.
- **Flood processes are kept together across physical and secondary evidence.**
  Questions 22, 23, 24 and 26 connect geology, antecedent conditions, basin
  shape and the role of a wider flood-risk map.
- **Prefix `MC-GG-RVF-` was checked across the three data files before use.**
