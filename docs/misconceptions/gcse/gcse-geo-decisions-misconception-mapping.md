## The organising idea: reasoning failure, not command word alone

Paper 3 questions use recurring command words, but the intervention must
target the reasoning step students omit:

- define a genuine geographical issue rather than answer a simple fact;
- use evidence across the booklet and reference it;
- balance alternatives, weight criteria and justify the final choice;
- evaluate time, uncertainty, reversibility, scale and stakeholder interests;
- assess provenance and reconcile conflicting sources;
- synthesise figures and transfer wider geographical knowledge only when it is
  relevant to the decision.

## Grouping decisions worth knowing

- **Issue framing includes FB-03.** Categorising evidence as environmental,
  economic and social is part of recognising why an issue is contested; it is
  not a generic three-column sorting skill detached from the decision.
- **Evidence breadth is separate from justified conclusion.** Questions 02,
  10, 23 and FB-02 test whether the student uses the booklet. Questions 04, 11,
  22, 35 and 36 test whether that evidence is weighed into an actual judgement.
- **Time and uncertainty are one intervention.** Questions 05, 28, 29 and 32
  all punish treating an impact, forecast or source as fixed and certain.
- **Source provenance is separate from synthesis.** Questions 12, 21, 31 and
  FB-05 ask why author, purpose, method or date changes confidence. Questions
  17, 18 and 30 ask students to connect evidence into an explanation.
- **Prefix `MC-GG-DEC-` was checked across the three data files before use.**
