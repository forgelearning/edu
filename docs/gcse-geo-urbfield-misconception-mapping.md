Eleventh curated bank of the `gcse-geo` retag. The generated tags split the
Stratford enquiry into question-level slugs, so students with the same
fieldwork-design error could not be grouped for intervention.

## The organising idea: enquiry design and interpretation

The Stratford sites are useful because they contain contrasting land uses in a
small area, but the useful taxonomy is about how students design and interpret
urban fieldwork:

- hypotheses and conclusions must be testable and valid;
- regeneration is a theory about change and sustainability, not just new
  buildings;
- EQS, IMD, Census, footfall, noise and satisfaction measure different things;
- temporal sampling, questionnaire wording and respondent groups affect
  reliability;
- maps and graphs must match the data; anomalies need investigation rather than
  deletion.

## Grouping decisions worth knowing

- **Hypothesis and validity stay together.** Questions 01, 16, 18 and 32 all
  test whether students can link a measurable hypothesis to a cautious
  conclusion rather than claim causation from one visit.
- **EQS is separate from wider deprivation.** Questions 03, 11, 24, 26 and
  FB-01 distinguish visible environmental scoring from IMD/Census dimensions.
- **Questionnaire scales are separate from questionnaire quality.** Questions
  05, 12, 20 and FB-03 test ordinal, statement and semantic-differential
  formats; questions 19, 27 and 28 test leading wording and whose perspective
  is being sampled.
- **Evidence presentation groups graphs, maps and GIS.** Questions 07, 23 and
  30 test selecting a display that reveals comparison or spatial pattern, not
  merely naming a familiar graph.
- **Prefix `MC-GG-URF-` was checked across the three data files before use.**
