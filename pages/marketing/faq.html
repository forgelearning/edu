What Forge is

Forge is a web-based GCSE and A Level revision platform that gives students multiple-choice questions mapped to their exam board's specification, records the misconception tag attached to a wrong answer, and surfaces those patterns to both the student and their teacher. Where a human-readable descriptor is available, it is shown alongside the tag.

No. Forge does not use AI to generate questions, explanations, or feedback. Every question, every distractor, and every misconception tag has been written and reviewed by subject specialists against published examiners' reports.

The "intelligence" in Forge is in the question bank and the misconception taxonomy — not a language model. This matters because AI-generated questions can contain factual errors, reward the wrong reasoning, or drift away from what an examiner actually wants. Forge's content is fixed, auditable, and exam-board specific.

Most revision apps test whether a student knows the right answer. Forge is specifically designed around why students get things wrong. Wrong-answer options are linked to misconception tags based on specification content and examiners' reports; some newer or less common tags may still display their code until a plain-English descriptor is added.

When a student selects a wrong answer, Forge:

  • Identifies the specific misconception triggered
  • Shows a short explanation of exactly what went wrong
  • Offers a similar question to test whether the misconception has been corrected
  • Logs the pattern so the teacher can see it in their dashboard

Seneca and Quizlet do not surface misconception data to teachers. Forge does.

Forge currently has live question banks across GCSE, A Level, and IB subject areas, mapped to Edexcel, AQA, OCR, WJEC, Eduqas, and IB specifications. The catalogue is changing quickly, so the product's subject pages are the source of truth for what is live:

  • Economics (Edexcel 9EC0) · Psychology (AQA 7182) · Sociology (AQA 7192) · Geography (Edexcel 9GE0)
  • Business (Edexcel 9BS0) · Chemistry (Edexcel 9CH0) · Biology (Edexcel 9BI0) · Physics (AQA 7408)
  • History (AQA 7042) · Law (AQA 7162) · Criminology (WJEC) · Politics (Edexcel 9PL0)
  • Maths (Edexcel 9MA0) · German (Edexcel 9GN0) · French (Edexcel 9FR0) · Computer Science (Eduqas A500QS)
  • Religious Studies (Eduqas) · Health & Social Care (OCR H604) · English Literature · English Language & Literature · IB Mandarin

Coverage varies by subject: many GCSE and A Level subjects now have live banks, while other banks are still being checked or extended. Each subject page is the source of truth for its exam board, paper structure, topics and current bank status.

Question banks are being extended continuously — the catalogue should not be treated as a claim that every topic, option or coursework component is already covered.

What Forge does

The teacher dashboard gives class-level visibility without requiring teachers to mark or monitor individually:

  • Misconception heatmap — which spec points are firing most frequently across the class
  • Class overview — every student's accuracy, weakest topic, and top misconception, sorted by who is struggling most
  • Student drill-down — click any student to see their full misconception profile and repair history
  • Corrective starter generator — Forge proposes a warm-up activity based on the class's top three misconceptions, ready to drop into a lesson
  • Assignments — teachers can set specific banks as homework with a due date; the dashboard shows completed, in-progress and not-started students, question progress and accuracy
  • School Overview — a separate read-only view for SLT and Heads of Department showing cross-class trends, engagement rates, and a school-wide misconception league table

Forge is designed to augment teaching, not replace it. It works best as:

  • A structured homework or directed study task between lessons
  • A pre-lesson warm-up (the teacher sets a bank; students spend 5–10 minutes before the lesson starts)
  • Spaced retrieval practice in the weeks before exams
  • A diagnostic tool — teachers can see which misconceptions are live before planning a lesson

Forge does not deliver explanatory content, mark extended answers, or teach new material. The teacher remains the expert; Forge provides the data.

A typical workflow:

  1. Teacher sets a Forge bank as homework before a lesson (e.g. the Inflation bank before a lesson on monetary policy)
  2. Students complete questions at home — Forge logs every response
  3. Teacher opens the dashboard before the lesson and sees the class's top misconceptions
  4. Teacher uses the corrective starter to address the top-firing errors at the start of the lesson
  5. Students who are still firing misconceptions are flagged; teacher knows who to target

This loop closes the gap between what students think they know and what examiners reward.

Inclusion & access arrangements
Ofsted context: Forge was designed with inclusion as a core principle, not an afterthought. The sections below explain how Forge supports students with a range of needs and what is still being built.

Teachers can set a per-student extra time arrangement directly in the class overview. The options are Standard, +25% extra time, and +50% extra time. This setting affects Timed practice (the timed exam-pressure mode) — a student with +25% gets 375 seconds instead of 300.

The arrangement is:

  • Set by the teacher in the dashboard — the student does not need to self-declare or opt in
  • Visible to the student on the Timed practice ready screen ("your time arrangement has been applied")
  • Stored against the student's record and applied automatically on every subsequent session

The main quiz (Practice) is untimed — there is no clock and no penalty for taking longer. Only Timed practice has a timer, and it is an optional mode.

Forge is built in plain HTML with no canvas elements, so it is fully compatible with screen readers and browser-based accessibility tools. Students can:

  • Increase text size using browser zoom without breaking the layout
  • Use the browser's built-in reader mode on supported browsers
  • Switch to light mode for higher contrast if preferred
  • Use browser extensions such as Texthelp Read&Write, since the page is standard HTML
In development: A dedicated high-contrast mode and a dyslexia-friendly font toggle (OpenDyslexic) are on the near-term roadmap. Formal WCAG 2.1 AA audit is planned before wider rollout.

Several features are specifically designed to prevent low-attaining students from feeling penalised:

  • An explanation on every wrong answer — no student sees just "incorrect". Every wrong answer triggers a targeted explanation of what went wrong and why the right answer is right
  • Similar-question attempts — after a wrong answer, students get a second chance on a related question testing the same concept. This means every mistake is an opportunity rather than a dead end
  • Spaced repetition — questions the student has previously struggled with are weighted to appear more often, without the student knowing. The system works hardest on their weakest areas
  • Weekly class league — class-joined students see a league of XP earned that week within their own class, showing classmates as first name and last initial; it resets every Monday, teachers can switch it off, and no public or cross-school league is shown
  • Class friends — off by default and available only when the teacher enables it and everyone in the class has an individual private code. Students can then send requests to classmates; once both agree, each can see the other’s XP, answer count, accuracy and practice streak. There is no messaging, either student can remove the connection, and teachers can see the class’s connections and switch friends off

Currently, all question content is in English and there is no translation functionality. Students with EAL can use browser-based translation tools (such as Google Translate's page translation) as Forge is standard HTML — it does not block or disable translation extensions.

Planned: Glossary tooltips for subject-specific technical vocabulary are planned for the next development phase. These would give quick definitions of terms like "price elasticity" or "mens rea" on hover, reducing the vocabulary barrier without changing the question content.

Yes. Forge is a web application that runs in any modern browser — Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge — on any device including Chromebooks, iPads, Android tablets, phones, and laptops. There is nothing to install.

Students with a Pro or trial account can log in from any device with their email and password. Their data — responses, misconception history, and progress — is stored in the cloud and syncs across all devices automatically.

This is particularly relevant for students who do not have a personal device at home, as they can use a school Chromebook or library computer without losing their progress.

Forge requires an internet connection and does not currently offer an offline mode. For students without home internet access, we recommend:

  • Setting Forge sessions during school study periods or after-school provision where devices and wifi are available
  • Using school library or computer room time
Planned: A lightweight offline mode — allowing a pre-loaded bank to be completed without internet and synced when connectivity is restored — is under consideration for a future release.
Data protection & privacy
Summary: Forge collects only what it needs to function. It does not sell data, run advertising profiles, or share student information with third parties for commercial purposes. Public-page analytics is optional and consent-based. Full details are in the Privacy & DPA.

Forge is designed around UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Schools should review the current Privacy & DPA document as part of their own procurement and data-protection assessment. The key points are:

  • Data controller: Forge Education — see the Privacy & DPA document for current contact details
  • Legal basis: Contract (for Pro/trial accounts where a student has signed up) and Legitimate Interests (for class-joined students, where the school has a legitimate educational interest in tracking progress)
  • What is collected: Display name, question responses, misconception tags, and (for Pro/trial accounts) email address and hashed password. No home addresses, phone numbers, photographs, or government identifiers are collected
  • Data processors: Supabase for application data and authentication, Stripe for payments, Formspree for the waitlist, Google Fonts for font delivery, and Google Analytics 4 for consented public-page analytics
  • Retention: Student response data is retained for the academic year plus one additional year, then deleted. Account data is deleted within 30 days of a deletion request

A full Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is available for schools that require one. Contact forge.educator@gmail.com.

It depends on how they access Forge:

  • Class-joined students — enter a display name, their teacher's class code, and an individual private student code. Classmates may see the display name in the weekly class league (as first name and last initial) and, in full, if the student accepts them as a friend; no email address is required
  • Free-tier students — enter only a display name. No email, no account
  • Pro/trial subscribers — provide an email address and create a password. This is necessary to enable cross-device login and is stored securely via Supabase Auth (bcrypt hashed; neither Forge nor Supabase can recover a plaintext password)

For most school use cases — where students join via a class code — no email address is ever collected from students.

Application data is stored in Supabase's EU-West (Ireland) data centre. Public pages also use Google Analytics 4, subject to the consent choice described in the privacy policy.

Stripe (used for payment processing only — not for storing student response data) processes globally but operates under Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) for international transfers.

Forge does not sell student data, run advertising profiles, or use analytics to analyse student quiz responses. Analytics is limited to public-site usage.

For schools on a paid licence, a formal DPA is available and recommended. It covers the processing activities, sub-processors, data subject rights, breach notification procedures, and retention schedules.

For individual teachers using Forge informally (e.g. as a free trial), a DPA is not legally required if students use the class-linked flow only (no email collection). However, we recommend notifying students and parents that their display name and response data are stored in accordance with the privacy policy.

Contact forge.educator@gmail.com to request a DPA.

Safeguarding

No. Forge has no messaging, comment, or communication functionality. There is no way for a teacher to send a message to a student through Forge, and no way for students to communicate with each other or with their teacher through the platform.

The teacher dashboard shows student performance data. The student sees their own profile data. There is no channel between them within Forge.

Mostly. Students can only open their own profile and misconception data. Class-joined students can see the weekly class league, which shows classmates' first names and last initials with XP earned that week. Friends are off by default; a teacher can enable them only after every student in the class has an individual private code. A student who accepts a classmate as a friend shares their XP, answer count, accuracy and practice streak with that one classmate. If a student enters an inappropriate name, teachers can rename any student from the class overview in the teacher dashboard. The replacement display name is what appears in teacher views, the weekly class league and friends lists.

Teachers should ask students to use an appropriate classroom display name and follow their school's naming expectations.

Cost & procurement

Forge has three tiers:

  • Free tier — sets of 10 questions with no wait between sets (up to 40 a day). No account or class needed to begin; an email account can save progress across devices.
  • Pro (student, self-funded) — £2.99/month per student, includes a 7-day free trial with no card required. Unlimited questions, all modes, cross-device login. Students pay directly via Stripe
  • School licence — annual whole-class or whole-school pricing, invoiced to the school. Includes teacher dashboard, school overview, DPA, and priority content requests. Contact forge.educator@gmail.com for a quote

Teachers can use the teacher dashboard free of charge during the pilot period.

Yes. School licences are invoiced directly to the school — no individual student payment required. One invoice covers all students in the licence scope (a class, a year group, or whole-school).

Forge can provide a quote, a proforma invoice, and a supplier information form. Contact forge.educator@gmail.com to start the process. Most schools can process Forge as a low-value purchase without full tender, as annual costs typically fall below the competitive quotation threshold.
