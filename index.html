The Forge loop

How a wrong answer becomes a fix.

Flashcard apps show you the right answer and move on. Forge treats the wrong answer as the most useful data in the session — and works it, there and then.

01 · TRIGGER

The misconception fires

Every distractor is written from documented examiner-report errors and tagged to a specific misconception — not "wrong", but why it's wrong.

02 · SCAFFOLD

A 30-second correction

Instead of just revealing the answer, Forge shows a two-sentence scaffold that dismantles that exact logic trap while it's still hot.

03 · RE-FORGE

Prove it, reframed

A twin question tests the same concept in a new scenario, immediately — so the fix is understood, not memorised.

Corrective starters · Auto-generated Ready to use

Each high-frequency misconception pairs with a ready-made 5-minute starter activity — projected on the board, worked through as a class, targeting the exact error.

MC-INF-01 23 fires this week
Starter: "Is it deflation?"

Project this scenario:

"UK inflation falls from 6.1% to 1.8%. The BBC reports that 'the cost of living crisis is easing.' A student in your class says prices are now falling."

Task (2 mins): On your whiteboard, write whether prices are rising, falling, or stable — and the correct term for what has happened to inflation.

Reveal: Prices are still rising (inflation is positive at 1.8%). The rate has slowed — this is disinflation. Prices would only be falling if inflation were negative (deflation).

Economics · 2.1.2Calculation

The CPI index stands at 130 in 2025 and 133.9 in 2026.

What is the inflation rate for 2026?

3.0% — change over base, times 100. That's the whole method. ✦

Economics · 2.1.2Concept

UK inflation falls from 4.2% to 2.1%. A headline reads: "Prices tumble as inflation halves."

Which statement about this economy is correct?

Rising more slowly is disinflation, not deflation — most students miss this. ✦

Essential FAQs

The useful answers, before you start.

What makes Forge different from a revision guide?

Forge starts with the mistake, not just the topic. Each wrong answer is tied to a documented misconception, followed by a short correction and a fresh question to prove the fix.

Can students use it without joining a class?

Yes. Students can practise independently for free, or subscribe for unlimited questions and the full Anvil, Crucible and progress features.

Which subjects and exam boards are covered?

Forge covers selected GCSE and A Level subjects with the exam board and paper structure shown on each subject page. The subject directory marks what is live, early access or still building.

What do teachers see?

Teachers see class-level misconception patterns, student progress and ready-to-use corrective starters. The focus is what to teach next, not a leaderboard of logins.

How do schools protect student data?

School views are designed around aggregated, read-only reporting for leadership, with the data and privacy details set out in the school overview and privacy information.
