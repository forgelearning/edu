# GCSE Geography — Urbanising World misconception mapping

`GCSE-GEO-URB` contains 39 questions, including fill-blank coverage twins.
Before this pass, 33 tags were single-use for 27 questions (0.692 of the
bank). The bank now uses 10 shared misconception tags and has no single-use
tags. Across GCSE Geography, the subject moved from 61/640 single-use tags
(0.095) to 34/640 (0.053); the mechanical ratchet remains 0. The organising
idea is to group by the error students make about urban processes, evidence
and interventions, rather than by Mumbai or another case-study label.

## Grouping decisions worth knowing

- **Megacity growth and migration reality stay distinct.** Questions about
  natural increase and rural-urban migration test the components of growth;
  push and pull questions test why migration does not guarantee formal work or
  housing.
- **Informal work is not the same as unemployment.** The informal-economy
  group preserves the distinction between productivity and legal protection.
- **City-wide pressures are separate from intervention trade-offs.** Rapid
  growth can strain connected infrastructure across a city; redevelopment and
  bottom-up schemes test who decides, who benefits and whether livelihoods are
  protected.
- **Urban structure is split from quality of life.** Megacity, primacy and
  capital functions are definitions; accessibility, land use and quality of
  life require interpreting spatial evidence and multiple indicators.
- Prefix `MC-GG-URB-` was checked across `data/forge-data.js`,
  `data/misconception-labels.js` and `data/starter-activities.js` before use.
